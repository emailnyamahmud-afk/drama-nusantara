Cinematic realistic macro photography extreme close-up of a Yupa stone pillar from Kutai, Kalimantan Timur, abad 4-5 Masehi. Cylindrical stone pillar (approx 1.5m tall) with Pallawa/Brahmi Akhir script carved in Sanskrit poetry. The script is weathered but legible, recording King Mulawarman's generosity of giving 20,000 cows to Brahmana at Waprakeswara. Stone is grey andesite with reddish-brown patina from age, with moss and lichen growth in crevices. Base of pillar has flower offerings and ash from ritual fire. Background blurred showing tropical forest.

Setting: One of 7 Yupa at Muara Kaman, Kutai, Kalimantan Timur, abad 4-5 Masehi. Earliest script in Southeast Asia (Nasha Rodziadi Khaw). Sanskrit poetry in Pallawa script. Now in Museum Nasional Jakarta.

Lighting: warm 3000K raking side light from camera right (key light, revealing stone texture, script depth, and patina), low ambient fill. High contrast 5:1. Dust particles in light beam.

Mood: the dawn of writing in Nusantara - the earliest known script in Southeast Asia, recording the generosity of the first Hindu king of the archipelago.
