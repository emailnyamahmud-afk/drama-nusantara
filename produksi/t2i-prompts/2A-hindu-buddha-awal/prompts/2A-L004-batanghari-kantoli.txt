Cinematic realistic documentary landscape of hilir Sungai Batanghari, Kan-to-li kingdom (Jambi, abad 5-6 Masehi). Wide river Batanghari meandering through tropical lowland, with a bustling trading port at the riverbank. Bamboo and thatch market stalls, trading boats from India (with Indian merchants), Persia, and China (Tang dynasty) at dock. Candi bata (brick temple, early Muarajambi) visible on a slight elevation. Arca Nandi and Wisnu in a small shrine. Tropical forest, distant hills. Commodities: gold, camphor, frankincense, agarwood, damar resin, cinnamon. No modern elements.

Setting: Hilir Sungai Batanghari, Jambi, abad 5-6 Masehi. Center of Kan-to-li kingdom (455-563 M), active trading port on east coast Sumatra. Controlled 2 ports: Fo-shi (Palembang) and Malayu (Jambi). Rulers: Varanarendra, Saba-la-na-lin-da.

Lighting: warm 4000K late morning sun from camera left (key light, golden on river and market), cool 6500K sky fill from above right. Mid contrast 3:1. Sea haze, smoke from cooking fires.

Mood: cosmopolitan trading port, where India, Persia, and China met - the economic heart of early Nusantara, where Hindu and Buddhist cultures mingled with indigenous traditions.

Style: photorealistic bamboo, thatch, boat, and tropical foliage textures, 35mm film grain, deep depth of field f/8, 24mm wide lens equivalent, 8K hyperdetailed, color graded warm golden highlights cool blue shadows, National Geographic coastal documentary aesthetic.
