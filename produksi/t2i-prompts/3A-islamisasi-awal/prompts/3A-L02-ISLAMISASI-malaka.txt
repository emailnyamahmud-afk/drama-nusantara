Cinematic realistic documentary wide establishing shot of Malaka kingdom, Semenanjung Malaya, pesisir Selat Malaka, 15th century Masehi (peak era 1450-1500 M, before Portuguese conquest 1511 M). Greatest Islamic emporium in Southeast Asia — surpassed Pasai. Wide harbor (muara Sungai Malaka) with hundreds of trading vessels: huge Chinese junks (Cheng Ho era), Indian dhows, Javanese jong (largest in Nusantara), Malay perahu, Persian merchant ships. Sultan's istana (palace) on Bukit Melaka overlooking harbor — wooden Malay-Islamic palace on stilts with multi-tiered roof (atap limas bersusun) blending Malay tradition with emerging Islamic style, NOT Chinese pagoda. Bustling pasar at foot of hill with multiethnic traders: Gujarati, Bengal, Persian, Arab, Chinese (largest non-Muslim group), Javanese, Tamil, Burmese, Siamese. Islamic institutions: Masjid Agung Malaka (early Javanese-style atap tumpang + emerging Islamic minaret), qadi's office, Islamic school (madrasah). Bazaar with textiles from India, ceramics and silk from China, spices from Maluku, pepper from Sumatra, gold from Minangkabau. Currency: dirham (gold/silver local) + picis (Chinese copper kepeng). Ujong Tanjong lookout. Samudra Malaka visible in distance with monsoon-season dhows waiting at anchor. No people in foreground focus, but distant figures of traders, ulama, sultan's guards in istana courtyard.

Setting: Malaka — founded ±1400-1403 M by Parameswara (Srivijayan Hindu prince from Palembang). Ming China sent Yin Ch'ing 1403 M and Cheng Ho 1407 M for protection against Siam. Parameswara converted to Islam ±1414 M (age 72 per Pires), took name Sultan Iskandar Shah. China withdrew 1435 M → Malaka turned to Islam as new source of legitimacy + trade network. Surpassed Pasai as greatest Muslim port in Southeast Asia. Fell to Portuguese (Afonso de Albuquerque) 1511 M.

Lighting: warm 3500K late afternoon golden hour sun from camera left (key light, long golden shadows on harbor and istana rooftops), cool 6500K sky fill from above, mid contrast 3:1, sea mist.

Mood: cosmopolitan Islamic emporium at its zenith — the greatest Muslim trading port in Southeast Asia, where 84 languages were spoken (per Pires), where Islam, China, India, and Nusantara converged. End of an era — Malaka would fall to Portuguese 1511, ending the Islamisasi Awal period.
