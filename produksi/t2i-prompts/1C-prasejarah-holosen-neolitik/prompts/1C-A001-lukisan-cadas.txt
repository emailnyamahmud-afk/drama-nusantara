Cinematic realistic macro photography extreme close-up of pre-Austronesian rock art on limestone cave wall, showing two motifs: (1) negative hand stencil (cap tangan negatif) - red ochre sprayed around an outstretched hand, leaving hand silhouette in natural rock color, fingers spread including thumb; (2) animal figure (babi / pig) painted in red ochre, crude but recognizable with body, four legs, head, and tail. Both motifs on rough grey-beige limestone with natural texture, cracks, and weathering. Pigment aged with reddish-brown patina.

Setting: Cave wall in Karst Maros-Pangkep or Karst Sangkulirang-Mangkalihat, 40-20 thousand years old. Rock art tradition began 67.8 ka (Gua Metanduno) and continues through Pleistosen Akhir to Holosen. Hand stencils tradition widespread across Sulawesi, Kalimantan, Papua.

Lighting: warm 3000K raking side light from camera right (key light, revealing rock texture, hand stencil silhouette, pigment layering), low ambient fill. High contrast 5:1 for dramatic documentation of ancient art.

Mood: hands across time, the earliest signature of human creativity - a hand pressed against stone 40 thousand years ago, still visible today. Connection between ancient artist and modern viewer.
