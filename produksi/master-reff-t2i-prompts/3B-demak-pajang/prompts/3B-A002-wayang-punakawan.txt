Cinematic realistic macro photography of Wayang kulit Punakawan figures (Javanese-Islamic cultural tool), 16th century AD, Java — wayang puppets used by Sunan Kalijaga for Islamic teaching. 3-4 wayang kulit (shadow puppet) figures on dark background, displayed flat. Visible: Semar (the fat, wise servant), Gareng (the deformed philosopher), and Bagong (the simple truth-teller). Each puppet is approximately 50 cm tall, made of buffalo hide with buffalo horn handles (gapit), painted in gold, red, blue, and white. These are the Punakawan — the Javanese servants who are not in the Indian Mahabharata but were created by Javanese culture. Sunan Kalijaga used them to teach Islamic values through familiar Javanese characters.

Lighting: warm 2700K raking light from camera left (key light, revealing leather texture and paint), low ambient fill from a backlight screen (simulating wayang screen). High contrast 5:1.

Mood: Culture as dakwah — the Punakawan puppets, Sunan Kalijaga's method of spreading Islam. He didn't destroy wayang; he transformed it. Semar became the voice of Sufi wisdom. Through familiar characters, Islamic values reached Javanese commoners in their own language.

Style: photorealistic textures, 35mm film grain, shallow depth of field f/2.8, 100mm macro lens, 8K hyperdetailed, scientific documentation aesthetic. Aspect ratio 1:1 square. High detail, 4K, sharp focus.
