Cinematic realistic macro photography of Prasasti Nalanda (Sriwijaya-India diplomatic inscription), 860 AD, Nalanda, India — Sriwijaya's diplomatic gift to India. A copper plate inscription (3 plates, joined by copper ring). Visible: Sanskrit script in Nagari letters, recording King Devapala of the Pala dynasty granting 5 villages to support a monastery built by Balaputradewa of Sriwijaya at Nalanda. The inscription proves Sriwijaya's international diplomatic status — a Southeast Asian king building a monastery in India. Approximately 25 cm per plate.

Lighting: warm 3000K raking light from camera left (key light, revealing copper patina and script), low ambient fill. High contrast 5:1.

Mood: International diplomacy — the Nalanda inscription, proof that Sriwijaya was a global power. King Balaputradewa built a monastery for his students at Nalanda, India's greatest Buddhist university. A Southeast Asian king endowing an Indian institution — diplomacy through religion.

Style: photorealistic textures, 35mm film grain, shallow depth of field f/2.8, 100mm macro lens, 8K hyperdetailed, scientific documentation aesthetic. Aspect ratio 1:1 square. High detail, 4K, sharp focus.
