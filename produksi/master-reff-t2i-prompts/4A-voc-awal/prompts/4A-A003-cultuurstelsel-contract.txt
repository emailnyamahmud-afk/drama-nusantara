Cinematic realistic macro photography of Cultuurstelsel contract (Cultivation System indenture document, 1830+), 1830-1870 AD, Java — forced cultivation contract under Van den Bosch's system. A Dutch colonial administrative document on dark surface. Visible: official Dutch government form with printed headers (Koninklijk Besluit), handwritten entries in both Dutch and Javanese script. The document records a Javanese village's forced cultivation quota: how much land must be devoted to coffee/sugar/indigo, and how much must be delivered to the government warehouse. Stamped with the colonial government seal. Approximately 35 cm tall, yellowed paper.

Lighting: warm 3000K raking light from camera right (key light, revealing text and stamps), low ambient fill. High contrast 4:1.

Mood: The paper that enslaved Java — a Cultuurstelsel contract, the bureaucratic instrument of Van den Bosch's Cultivation System. This piece of paper determined how many days a Javanese farmer must work for the Dutch, how much of his land must grow export crops, and how little he would be paid. The system funded the Netherlands' industrialization while starving Java.
