Cinematic realistic macro photography of Keris Mataram (ceremonial dagger of Mataram sultans), 17th-18th century AD, Mataram, Central Java — symbol of Javanese kingship. A keris (ceremonial dagger) on dark velvet. Visible: wavy blade (luk 13 — 13 curves, highest number, reserved for royalty), pamor pattern (meteoric iron damascene showing 'Beras Wutah' motif), gold hilt (ukiran) with Javanese floral design, gold sheath (wrangka) with pendok (gold cover). The keris is the ultimate symbol of Javanese kingship — combining Islamic authority (keris as symbol of faith) with pre-Islamic mystical tradition (keris as pusaka/sacred heirloom). Approximately 40 cm long.

Lighting: warm 2700K raking light from camera left (key light, revealing pamor damascene pattern and gold detail), low ambient fill. High contrast 5:1.

Mood: The soul of kingship — the keris of Mataram, where steel, gold, and mysticism meet. The wavy blade represents the naga (serpent) of pre-Hindu belief; the pamor pattern holds magical power; the gold hilt proclaims royal authority. In Mataram, the keris was both weapon and spiritual object — a symbol that bridged Islamic faith and Javanese mysticism.
