Cinematic realistic macro photography of Kalender Islam-Jawa (Sultan Agung's calendar reform, 1633), 1633 AD, Mataram, Central Java — Sultan Agung's synthesis of Islamic and Javanese calendars. A traditional Javanese calendar (primbon) on dark surface. Visible: combination of Islamic month names (Muharram, Safar, etc.) with Javanese pasaran days (Legi, Pahing, Pon, Wage, Kliwon) and the 7-day week (Saptawara). The calendar shows the fusion of Islamic lunar months with Javanese cyclical days. Hand-written on brown paper with Javanese numerals. Sultan Agung created this hybrid calendar in 1633 (Saka 1555) as a statement of Islamic-Javanese identity — neither purely Islamic nor purely Javanese, but both.

Lighting: warm 2700K raking light from camera left (key light, revealing handwriting and paper texture), low ambient fill. High contrast 4:1.

Mood: Two calendars, one identity — Sultan Agung's calendar reform of 1633 created a uniquely Javanese Islamic calendar that combined Islamic lunar months with Javanese pasaran days. This calendar is still used in Java today — a living legacy of Sultan Agung's vision of Islam and Javanese culture as inseparable.
